Safari Bermuda shorts: sewing guide

Guide in progress. It is still being written and may change in the coming days.

Guide for

Before you start

The safari Bermuda shorts are workwear shorts: sturdy, comfortable, with pockets that really work. Wear them with the safari jacket or on their own. The fit comes from FreeSewing’s Titan trouser block, to which we added a straight waistband with belt loops, a button fly, side seam pockets, a choice of cargo and back pockets, and a cuff or a hem.

Trousers are more demanding than a jacket: a few millimetres off in the vertical measurements will show in the crotch. Two pieces of advice before anything else:

  • Take all vertical measurements from the same waistline. Tie a string around your waist before measuring, and always start from it.
  • Make a toile in paper or in cheap fabric (an old bed sheet, for instance). For shorts, the toile can be just the Back and Front, tacked together with long stitches: that is enough to check the crotch and the width at the seat.

The guide is the same for men and for women, except for the side of the fly: the fly facing goes on the leftright front as worn. Choose “Men” or “Women” above: the fly and waistband steps adapt.

What you need

  • Main fabric, 140 cm (55") wide, folded in half: from 1.10 m (1 ¼ yd) for a small size without cargo pockets, to 2.20 m (2 ½ yd) for a very large size with the cargo and back pockets. For an average size, allow 1.40 to 1.60 m (1 ⅝ to 1 ¾ yd). These amounts come from laying the pieces out on the fabric, seam allowances included: add 10 to 20 cm (4 to 8 in) for pre-washing and pattern matching. The “Create a pattern” page gives the exact amount for your measurements and options.
  • Pocketing (optional): about 0.30 m of lightweight cotton, if you prefer pocket bags thinner than the main fabric.
  • Fusible interfacing: about 0.50 m, for the waistband and the fly.
  • 15 mm buttons: 3 for the fly, 1 per pocket flap.
  • One 18 to 20 mm button for the waistband.
  • Stay tape (or a 1 cm strip of interfacing): about 40 cm, for the side pocket openings.
  • Matching thread, and slightly heavier thread for topstitching if you like.

Fabric

Summer: lightweight cotton drill, linen or a linen-cotton blend, heavy poplin. Linen is wonderful in hot weather, but it creases a lot: your shorts will soon look lived-in. That is a style, not a flaw.

Spring and autumn: heavy drill, cotton gabardine, twill. This is the original: the khaki drill of colonial-era uniforms.

Avoid very drapey fabrics: the fly, the bellows pockets and the cuff need a fabric that holds a crease. Avoid stretch fabrics too: the pattern is not designed for them.

Transfer all the markings carefully, on the wrong side:

  • on the Back: the side pocket openings, the top and bottom of the cargo pocket, the top of the back pocket;
  • on the Front: the side pocket openings, the cargo pocket, the J-stitch of the fly, and the pleats if you chose them;
  • at the bottom of the Back and Front: the hemline, the cuff fold line and the turn-under line;
  • on the waistbands: the fold line, the side seam, the centre front and the belt loop positions;
  • on the fly facing: the buttonholes; on the fly shield: the buttons.

Tip. Also mark the wrong side of each Front with a small cross: on drill, the right and wrong sides look very much alike.

1. Finish the edges

Overlock (or zigzag) all edges of the Back and Front except the top, which will be caught in the waistband. Finish the curved edge of the pocket bags too.

Finish the bottom of the legs as well: the 2 cm turn-under will be neater.

2. Fuse the interfacing

  • The waistband interfacing goes on the outer half of each waistband, the half that shows once the waistband is folded.
  • The fly facing interfacing goes on one half of the fly facing.
  • The fly shield interfacing goes on one half of the fly shield.

3. Make the pleats (option)

If you chose pleats, fold each pleat on the right side of the Front: bring the solid line (fold) onto the dashed line, so that the pleats face towards the fly. Baste them 0.5 cm from the edge, within the waist seam allowance.

Tip. Don’t press the pleats flat along their whole length: they should open freely below the waistband. Pressing the top 5 cm is enough.

4. Attach the back pockets

Attach the back pockets now, while the pieces are still flat. Make them following the page for the patch pockets you chose. Align the top of the pocket with the "Top of back pocket" marking, parallel to the waist.

If your pockets have flaps, sew the flap above the pocket, at the distance given in the pocket guide.

5. Make the side seam pockets and close the side seams

  1. Apply stay tape on the wrong side of the Front and Back, along the pocket opening, straddling the seam line. The edge is slightly on the bias there: without tape, it stretches with wear.
  2. Sew a pocket bag to the side seam of the Front, right sides together, between the two opening marks, 0.8 cm from the edge. Do the same with the other pocket bag on the Back.
  3. Turn the pocket bags to the inside, and understitch 0.3 cm from the seam, on the pocket bag side: the seam won’t roll to the outside.
  4. Place the Front on the Back, right sides together, and sew the side seam in two parts: from the waist to the top opening mark, then from the bottom mark to the bottom of the leg. The opening stays free.
  5. Sew the two pocket bags together all around, then finish the edge.
  6. Press the side seam open.
  7. Sew a bar tack at the top and bottom of the opening, across the seam: that is where the pocket takes the most strain.

The top of the pocket bag stays flat against the top of the Front: it will be caught in the waistband.

Watch out. If you chose pleats, the side seam of the Front is slightly longer than that of the Back, by about 3 mm. Ease in this small difference while pinning, without pulling on the fabric.

6. Attach the cargo pockets

The cargo pockets straddle the side seam. They must go on now, while the leg is still open and lies flat.

Make them following the page for the patch pockets you chose. Centre the pocket on the side seam, its top on the "Top of cargo pocket" marking and its bottom on the "Bottom of cargo pocket" marking. The pocket size has been calculated by the site for your measurements.

Tip. For a pocket with side bellows, sew the bellows on the Back side first: that is the side that takes the most volume when you sit down.

7. Close the inseam

Place the Front on the Back, right sides together, and sew the inseam, from the bottom of the leg to the crotch point. Press the seam open.

Do the same for the other leg. You now have two separate legs.

8. Close the crotch seam

Turn one leg right side out and slip it into the other, still wrong side out: the two legs are right sides together. Match the inseams at the crotch point.

Sew the crotch seam from the centre back, at the waist, to the bottom of the fly opening on the Front, where the J-stitch ends. Stitch a second time over the curve of the crotch: it is the seam that takes the most strain in the shorts.

Don’t close the centre front above that point: it is the fly opening.

9. Make the fly

This is the trickiest step. Take your time, and try it on the toile first if it is your first button fly.

The fly facing goes on the leftright front as worn, the fly shield on the rightleft front.

Prepare the fly facing

  1. Fold the fly facing in half lengthwise, right sides together. Sew the rounded bottom, clip the curve, turn and press.
  2. Make the 3 buttonholes now, on the markings: horizontal, 1.8 cm long, starting 1 cm from the seam line on the raw-edge side (that is 2 cm from the raw edge, with the 1 cm seam allowance). Buttonholes are much easier to get right on the fly facing alone than on the finished shorts.

Attach the fly facing

  1. Place the fly facing on the right side of the leftright front, raw edges on the centre front edge, and sew 1 cm from the edge, from the waist to the bottom of the opening.
  2. Turn the fly facing to the inside. Press, letting the front edge roll 1 to 2 mm past it: the facing must not show from the outside.
  3. Sew the J-stitch on the right side, following the marking, 3.5 cm from the centre front. It holds the facing. Check on the wrong side that it catches the facing along its whole length, and that it runs beside the buttonholes without touching them.

Attach the fly shield

  1. Fold the fly shield in half lengthwise, right sides together. Sew the bottom, turn and press.
  2. Place it under the rightleft front, raw edges on the centre front edge, and sew 1 cm from the edge. Press the seam allowance towards the fly shield and edgestitch the front edge at 0.2 cm.
  3. Close the fly, leftright front over rightleft front, centre fronts on top of each other. Mark the buttons through the buttonholes, then sew them onto the fly shield.
  4. Sew a bar tack at the bottom of the fly, across the J-stitch, through all layers.

Tip. To get the bottom of the fly nice and flat, press it on a tailor’s ham or a rolled-up towel, with the fly closed.

10. Prepare the belt loops

  1. Fold the belt loop strip in three lengthwise: one third towards the middle, then the other third over it. Press.
  2. Edgestitch both long edges at 0.2 cm.
  3. Cut the strip into 7 belt loops, on the lines.
  4. Baste the bottom of each loop to the right side of the shorts, at the waist, right sides together, the loop pointing down. The positions are marked on the waistband:
    • one on the centre back seam;
    • one on each side, halfway across the back;
    • one on each side seam;
    • one on each side, halfway across the front.

The loops will be caught in the waistband seam, then folded up in the next step.

11. Attach the waistband

  1. Sew the two waistbands together at the centre back, right sides together, with the 2 cm seam allowance. Press the seam open. This allowance lets you take in or let out the waist later.
  2. Pin the interfaced edge of the waistband to the waist of the shorts, right sides together. Match the centre back seam, the side seam marks and the centre fronts:
    • on the fly side, the waistband stops at the edge of the fly facing (centre front leftright);
    • on the button-tab side, it extends 4 cm past the centre front rightleft, above the fly shield.
  3. Sew 1 cm from the edge. The pocket bags, the pleats and the bottom of the belt loops are caught in the seam. Press the seam allowance towards the waistband.
  4. Close both ends: fold the waistband right sides together along its fold line, sew each end, trim the corners and turn.
  5. Turn under the seam allowance of the free edge. Pin the waistband on the wrong side, just covering the attaching seam. On the right side, stitch in the ditch of the attaching seam: the stitching doesn’t show and catches the waistband on the inside.
  6. Fold each belt loop up over the waistband. Turn the top under by 1 cm and bar tack it level with the top of the waistband.

Tip. The belt loops should be slightly longer than the waistband is high: that way, a thick leather belt slides through without forcing.

12. Make the cuff, or the hem

The three lines at the bottom of the pattern are used in both cases: the hemline, the cuff fold line 3 cm below it, and the turn-under line 3 cm further down. The bottom of the piece is 2 cm below the turn-under line.

Outside cuff (default)

  1. Fold the edge 2 cm to the wrong side, on the turn-under line. Press.
  2. Fold to the wrong side on the cuff fold line. Press, and sew the turned-under edge by hand with a blind stitch or by machine close to its edge.
  3. Turn the bottom of the leg out, on the hemline. You get a 3 cm cuff.
  4. Secure the cuff with a small tack on the side seam and on the inseam: it won’t unroll in the wash.

Hem (option)

Same lines, same first two folds: instead of turning the bottom out, fold it one last time to the inside, on the hemline. You get a neat 3 cm hem, folded twice. Sew it with a blind stitch or topstitch 2.8 cm from the bottom.

13. Finish

  1. Make the waistband buttonhole, horizontal, at the fly-side end, 1.5 cm from the edge.
  2. Sew the waistband button on the button-tab end, opposite the buttonhole.
  3. Sew on the pocket flap buttons.
  4. Give a final press, creasing the legs if you like.

And there you go: your safari Bermuda shorts are done. Well done: trousers are never a small job.

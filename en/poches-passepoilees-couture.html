Welt pocket: sewing guide

This guide explains how to sew all five pocket types: each type is described in full, step by step, with no cross-references. The pattern can be downloaded from the Welt pocket page.

The double welt pocket is also called a jetted pocket in the UK.

The pocket pieces (names printed on the pattern):

  • front pocket bag: the piece that lies against the wrong side of the garment;
  • turned front pocket bag: the same piece extended 2 cm upwards, for the slit pocket with or without flap;
  • back pocket bag: the piece that carries the facing, the one you see through the opening;
  • facing: a strip of main fabric sewn on the back pocket bag, so the lining does not show;
  • welt(s), reinforcement, flap, and the paper frame template used for marking.

Double welt

Step 1: Prepare and mark

  1. Cut the pieces for your pocket type (see the options guide).
  2. Fuse the reinforcement to the wrong side of the garment, centred on the pocket position using its notches.
  3. Fuse the interfacing to the welts.
  4. Sew the facing to the right side of the back pocket bag, at the top, lower edge turned under.
  5. Place the frame template on the right side of the garment and trace the frame inside the window, then the cutting line and the Y cuts.

Step 2: Place the welts, check and trace

  1. Fold each welt in half lengthwise, wrong sides together. Press.
  2. Place both welts on the right side of the garment:
    • folds touching each other on the cutting line;
    • raw edges facing outwards;
    • the strips extend 2 cm beyond each end of the frame.
  3. Put the frame template back on top and check that the folds fall on its centre mark.
  4. Trace the two stitching lines along the long edges of the window.

Step 3: Stitch the welts

  1. Stitch along each traced line, exactly from one end of the frame to the other.
  2. Backstitch at each end.
  3. Check on the wrong side that both lines of stitching start and end at the same point. Otherwise the corners of the opening will be crooked.

Step 4: Cut

  1. On the right side, fold the welt folds outwards. Their outer edges stay in place, held by the stitching.
  2. Cut the garment and the reinforcement along the cutting line, between the stitching lines, stopping short of the ends.
  3. Cut the Y to the last stitch of each line, as close as possible, without cutting it.
  4. Do not cut the welts.

Step 5: Turn through

  1. Push the welts and the Y triangles through the opening to the wrong side. The garment seam allowances, above and below the slit, turn with them.
  2. On the right side, adjust the lips: their folds meet in the centre and they are the same width.
  3. On the wrong side, pull the triangles gently to get neat corners.
  4. Press.

Step 6: Sew the front pocket bag

  1. On the wrong side, place the top of the front pocket bag on the free edge of the welt on the bag side (the lower one when the opening is horizontal).
  2. Sew them together without catching the garment.
  3. Fold the piece down and press the seam.

Step 7: Topstitch the sides and bottom

  1. On the right side, topstitch both sides and the bottom 3 to 4 mm from the frame.
  2. The topstitching catches the tips of the triangles and the front pocket bag.
  3. Leave the top free: the back pocket bag is not in place yet.
  4. Backstitch at both top corners.

Step 8: Sew the back pocket bag

  1. On the wrong side, place the top of the back pocket bag on the free edge of the other welt (the upper one when the opening is horizontal), facing towards the opening.
  2. Sew them together without catching the garment.
  3. Fold the piece down over the front pocket bag and press the seam.

Step 9: Topstitch the top

  1. On the right side, topstitch the top 3 to 4 mm from the frame, through the back pocket bag.
  2. Start and end this topstitching exactly on the side topstitching, to get a continuous frame.
  3. Backstitch.

Step 10: Assemble the pocket

  1. On the wrong side, fold the garment aside to free the pocket.
  2. Sew the front and back pocket bags together all around, with a normal seam.
  3. If your fabric frays, overlock or zigzag the edges together.
  4. Press the finished pocket.

Single welt

Step 1: Prepare and mark

  1. Cut the pieces for your pocket type (see the options guide).
  2. Fuse the reinforcement to the wrong side of the garment, centred on the pocket position using its notches.
  3. Fuse the interfacing to the welt.
  4. Sew the facing to the right side of the back pocket bag, at the top, lower edge turned under.
  5. Place the frame template on the right side of the garment and trace the frame inside the window, then the cutting line and the Y cuts.

Step 2: Place the welt, check and trace

  1. Fold the welt in half lengthwise, wrong sides together. Press.
  2. Place it on the right side, on the bag side:
    • raw edges on the cutting line;
    • fold pointing down;
    • the strip extends 2 cm beyond each end of the frame.
  3. Put the frame template back on top and check that the raw edges fall on its centre mark.
  4. Trace both long edges of the window: the lower one on the welt, the upper one on the garment.

Step 3: Stitch

  1. Stitch along both traced lines, exactly from one end of the frame to the other:
    • the lower line through the welt;
    • the upper line through the garment only.
  2. Backstitch at each end.
  3. Check on the wrong side that both lines start and end at the same point.

Step 4: Cut

  1. On the right side, fold the raw edges of the welt down over its stitching, to free the garment between the two lines.
  2. Cut the garment and the reinforcement along the cutting line, stopping short of the ends.
  3. Cut the Y to the last stitch of each line, as close as possible, without cutting it.
  4. Do not cut the welt.

Step 5: Turn through

  1. Push the welt, the Y triangles and the upper seam allowance through the opening to the wrong side.
  2. On the right side, adjust the lip: its fold reaches the upper edge, just beyond the garment stitching, and it fills the whole opening.
  3. On the wrong side, pull the triangles gently to get neat corners.
  4. Press.

Step 6: Sew the front pocket bag

  1. On the wrong side, place the top of the front pocket bag on the free edge of the welt.
  2. Sew them together without catching the garment.
  3. Fold the piece down and press the seam.

Step 7: Topstitch the sides and bottom

  1. On the right side, topstitch both sides and the bottom 3 to 4 mm from the frame.
  2. The topstitching catches the tips of the triangles and the front pocket bag.
  3. Leave the top free.
  4. Backstitch at both top corners.

Step 8: Place the back pocket bag

  1. On the wrong side, lay the back pocket bag over the front pocket bag, facing towards the opening, its top edge at least 2 cm above the slit.
  2. Pin or baste it, pin heads pointing up.
  3. Turn the garment right side out.

Step 9: Topstitch the top

  1. On the right side, topstitch the top 3 to 4 mm from the frame. The topstitching goes through the garment, the upper seam allowance and the back pocket bag: it is what holds the back pocket bag.
  2. Start and end this topstitching exactly on the side topstitching.
  3. Backstitch and remove the pins.

Step 10: Assemble the pocket

  1. On the wrong side, fold the garment aside to free the pocket.
  2. Sew the front and back pocket bags together all around, with a normal seam.
  3. If your fabric frays, overlock or zigzag the edges together.
  4. Press the finished pocket.

Single welt with flap

Step 1: Prepare and mark

  1. Cut the pieces for your pocket type (see the options guide).
  2. Fuse the reinforcement to the wrong side of the garment, centred on the pocket position using its notches.
  3. Fuse the interfacing to the welt.
  4. Sew the facing to the right side of the back pocket bag, at the top, lower edge turned under.
  5. Place the frame template on the right side of the garment and trace the frame inside the window, then the cutting line and the Y cuts.
  6. Prepare the flap:
    • fuse the flap interfacing;
    • sew the flap and its lining right sides together, leaving the top edge open;
    • trim the corners, turn and press;
    • topstitch 3 to 4 mm from the edge, stopping at the top seam allowance;
    • if it buttons, make the buttonhole.

Step 2: Place the welt, check and trace

  1. Fold the welt in half lengthwise, wrong sides together. Press.
  2. Place it on the right side, on the bag side:
    • raw edges on the cutting line;
    • fold pointing down;
    • the strip extends 2 cm beyond each end of the frame.
  3. Put the frame template back on top and check that the raw edges fall on its centre mark.
  4. Trace both long edges of the window: the lower one on the welt, the upper one on the garment.

Step 3: Stitch

  1. Stitch along both traced lines, exactly from one end of the frame to the other:
    • the lower line through the welt;
    • the upper line through the garment only.
  2. Backstitch at each end.
  3. Check on the wrong side that both lines start and end at the same point.

Step 4: Cut

  1. On the right side, fold the raw edges of the welt down over its stitching, to free the garment between the two lines.
  2. Cut the garment and the reinforcement along the cutting line, stopping short of the ends.
  3. Cut the Y to the last stitch of each line, as close as possible, without cutting it.
  4. Do not cut the welt.

Step 5: Turn through

  1. Push the welt, the Y triangles and the upper seam allowance through the opening to the wrong side.
  2. On the right side, adjust the lip: its fold reaches the upper edge, just beyond the garment stitching, and it fills the whole opening.
  3. On the wrong side, pull the triangles gently to get neat corners.
  4. Press.

Step 6: Sew the front pocket bag

  1. On the wrong side, place the top of the front pocket bag on the free edge of the welt.
  2. Sew them together without catching the garment.
  3. Fold the piece down and press the seam.

Step 7: Topstitch the sides and bottom

  1. On the right side, topstitch both sides and the bottom 3 to 4 mm from the frame.
  2. The topstitching catches the tips of the triangles and the front pocket bag.
  3. Leave the top free.
  4. Backstitch at both top corners.

Step 8: Slide in the flap and place the back pocket bag

  1. On the right side, slide the flap seam allowance into the opening, under the upper edge. The flap hangs over the opening. Centre and pin it.
  2. On the wrong side, the flap seam allowance lies on the upper seam allowance of the slit.
  3. Lay the back pocket bag on top, facing towards the opening, its top edge at least 2 cm above the slit.
  4. Pin or baste everything, then turn right side out.

Step 9: Topstitch the top

  1. On the right side, keep the flap down over the opening.
  2. Topstitch the top 3 to 4 mm from the frame, through the garment, the flap seam allowance and the back pocket bag.
  3. Start and end this topstitching on the side topstitching.
  4. Remove the pins.

Step 10: Assemble the pocket

  1. On the wrong side, fold the garment aside to free the pocket.
  2. Sew the front and back pocket bags together all around, with a normal seam.
  3. If your fabric frays, overlock or zigzag the edges together.
  4. Press the finished pocket.

Step 11: Sew on the button

If the flap buttons, sew the button on the mark, through the garment only, without catching the pocket. Otherwise the pocket can no longer open.

Slit pocket with flap

Step 1: Prepare and mark

  1. Cut the pieces for your pocket type (see the options guide).
  2. The turned front pocket bag edges the slit, and its inner face shows when the pocket opens. If your main fabric is light, cut this piece from main fabric: it is the simplest and avoids bulk. If it is heavy, cut it from lining and sew a main-fabric facing on it, around the slit position.
  3. Sew the facing to the right side of the back pocket bag, at the top, lower edge turned under.
  4. Fuse the reinforcement to the wrong side of the garment, centred using its notches.
  5. Prepare the flap:
    • fuse the flap interfacing;
    • sew the flap and its lining right sides together, leaving the top edge open;
    • trim the corners, turn and press;
    • topstitch 3 to 4 mm from the edge, stopping at the top seam allowance;
    • if it buttons, make the buttonhole.
  6. Place the frame template on the right side and trace the narrow frame, the cutting line and the Y cuts.

Step 2: Place the turned front pocket bag and trace

  1. Place the turned front pocket bag on the right side of the garment, right sides together. Centre it on the frame, its top edge 2 cm above the frame. To position it, use the notches of the reinforcement, or transfer the frame centre to the edge of the piece with two pins.
  2. Pin it.
  3. Place the frame template on the turned front pocket bag, aligned with the frame, and trace the outline of the window, the cutting line and the Y cuts.

Step 3: Stitch

  1. Stitch all around the traced frame, through the turned front pocket bag and the garment.
  2. Start in the middle of a long side. At the corners, pivot with the needle down.
  3. Finish by overlapping a few stitches.
  4. Shorten the stitch length: the corners are very close together, and this stitching makes the slit neat.

Step 4: Cut

  1. Cut through the turned front pocket bag, the garment and the reinforcement along the cutting line, stopping short of the ends.
  2. Cut the Y to the four corners of the stitching, as close as possible, without cutting the stitches. Use very sharp, pointed scissors.

Step 5: Turn through

  1. Push the turned front pocket bag through the slit to the wrong side. The garment seam allowances and the triangles turn with it.
  2. Press, rolling the seam 1 mm towards the wrong side, so that the front pocket bag does not show on the right side.
  3. The slit now forms a small, neat window.

Step 6: Topstitch the sides and bottom

  1. On the right side, topstitch both sides and the bottom 3 to 4 mm from the slit.
  2. The topstitching catches the tips of the triangles and the turned front pocket bag.
  3. Leave the top free to slide in the flap.
  4. Backstitch at both top corners.

Step 7: Slide in the flap and place the back pocket bag

  1. On the right side, slide the flap seam allowance into the slit, under the upper edge. The flap hangs over the slit. Centre and pin it.
  2. On the wrong side, the flap seam allowance comes out through the slit and lies on the turned front pocket bag.
  3. Lay the back pocket bag on top, facing towards the slit, its top edge at least 2 cm above.
  4. Pin or baste everything, then turn right side out.

Step 8: Topstitch the top

  1. On the right side, keep the flap down over the slit.
  2. Topstitch the top 3 to 4 mm from the slit, through the garment, the flap seam allowance, the turned front pocket bag and the back pocket bag.
  3. Start and end this topstitching on the side topstitching.
  4. Remove the pins.

Step 9: Assemble the pocket

  1. On the wrong side, fold the garment aside to free the pocket.
  2. Sew the front and back pocket bags together all around, with a normal seam.
  3. If your fabric frays, overlock or zigzag the edges together.
  4. Press the finished pocket.

Step 10: Sew on the button

If the flap buttons, sew the button on the mark, through the garment only, without catching the pocket. Otherwise the pocket can no longer open.

Tips

  • The narrower the lip, the more precisely you need to stitch and fold: the slightest irregularity shows, and the corners leave less room to cut accurately.
  • With a contrasting welt, cut the facing from the same fabric: it is what you see between the lips.
  • With a fabric that frays, stop the Y cuts one thread short of the stitching and handle the opening as little as possible.
  • As soon as the opening is angled, check the side (left or right pocket) before tracing with the frame template.

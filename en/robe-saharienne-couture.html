Safari dress: sewing and assembly guide

Guide in progress. It is still being developed and may change in the coming days.

This guide gives the assembly order of the safari dress, from cutting to the belt. It follows the safari jacket guide: where a step is the same, it refers to the simple or lined safari jacket guide. Always make a test first, in paper or in a cheap fabric (an old sheet, for example), before cutting the fabric chosen for the garment.

Supplies

  • Main fabric, 140 cm wide, about:
    • mini, short sleeves: 2.30 m;
    • midi, short sleeves: 2.80 m;
    • long sleeves: add 0.50 m.
    • Summer: a light fabric, linen, light cotton, seersucker.
    • Mid-season: a sturdier fabric, gabardine, heavy drill, wool twill.
  • Lining (lined version): about 1.20 m, viscose or fine cotton. It stops at the hips.
  • Iron-on interfacing: about 0.50 m, for the collar and the facings.
  • Buttons, 1.5 cm across: about 7 for the mini and 9 for the midi, down the front. Add 1 button per pocket flap, 2 for the epaulettes and 2 for the sleeve tabs.
  • Matching thread.

These yardages are estimates: they depend on the size and the options.

1. Cutting

After cutting the pieces, mark the notches carefully:

  • Left front and right front. To avoid ending up with two left fronts or two right fronts, lay the fronts face to face, buttonhole and button lines against each other. For a dress (women’s buttoning), mark the buttonholes on the right front and the buttons on the left front, then mark the front of each piece with a distinctive sign.
  • The hem fold lines, front and back, and on the facings.
  • The front and back waist darts, and the bust dart.
  • The pocket positions, as you choose and to your dimensions.
  • The top and bottom armhole marks on Sleeve 1 and Sleeve 2.
  • A centre mark on the collar, at the centre fold, on the lower (less curved) edge.
  • On the front piece, the top mark of the button stand.
  • The top of the slits, if you chose them.

Lined version: also mark the centre of the lining collar, and the armhole marks on Lining sleeve 1 and Lining sleeve 2.

The belt and belt loops are only added to the pattern if you chose the tie belt.

2. Finishing the edges

Simple version: finish the same pieces as for the simple safari jacket (front, back, facing, sleeves), except the hem along the fold.

Lined version: only the lower part of the dress is finished (front, back, and side seams below the lining), since the lining stops at the hips.

3. Interfacing

  • The facing interfacing goes on the facing (one piece and its mirror piece).
  • The collar interfacing goes on the collar piece that stays visible once the collar is in place and folded.

Interfacing is cut without seam allowance.

4. Sewing and assembly

Folds and small pieces

  • Sew the cuff fold, if you chose long sleeves (fold at 2 cm, seam at 1.5 cm), then assemble the cuffs, sides (a) together, sides (b) together.
  • Sew the collar on three sides, leaving the lower edge open, then turn it right side out. Topstitching the three sides gives a better finish.
  • Prepare the epaulettes, sleeve tabs and pocket flaps as shown on their pages.

Sleeves

As for the safari jacket:

  • Short sleeves. The hem is a double-fold hem: two dotted lines are drawn on the pattern. First fold the raw edge 1.5 cm to the wrong side, then fold again 3 cm, and topstitch close to the first fold.
  • Long sleeves. Assemble the sleeve pieces, then the cuffs, as for the safari jacket.
  • If you add sleeve pockets, now is the time: it will be harder later.

Back

  • Sew the back darts. They are diamond darts: they continue below the waist.

Front

  • Sew the bust dart, then the waist darts, before placing the pockets.
  • Position your pockets (the placement shown on the pattern is a guide) and sew them on. On the dress, the chest pockets stop at the bust line.
  • Sew the facings to the fronts partway, stopping at the top mark of the button stand.
  • Turn right side out and press the fold well. No topstitching yet: if you want it, do it after attaching the collar.
  • Make the buttonholes. They are vertical, on the centre front, with the same spacing as on a jacket, about 10 cm.

Front and back: the body

  • Lined version with shoulder yoke: first place the yoke on the back and on the fronts.
  • Join the back to the right front and the left front: shoulders, then sides.
  • With side slits: stop the side seam at the top mark of the slit (12 cm above the hem). Press the allowances open, fold them to the wrong side along the slit and topstitch.
  • Belt loops (if you chose the belt): cut the loop strip in two. Slip a loop into each side seam, centred on the waist, before stitching it. There is no notch on the pattern: find the waist on yourself or on the test garment.
  • If you planned topstitching, topstitch the shoulder seam and the side seams towards the back.
  • Press the hem fold well.

Back vent

The back vent is made with the Outer vent and Inner vent pieces, as for the lined safari jacket: follow the photos in its guide. It is 12 cm long on the mini and 18 cm on the midi.

Hem

  • Tuck the "tabs" that stick out beyond the fold of the front pieces under the facings.
  • Fold the hem: first the 2 cm turn-under, then the 3.5 cm hem, on the dashed fold line. Stitch close to the first fold.

Body and collar

  • To avoid any confusion, the easiest is to watch the video in the safari jacket guide: attaching the collar (from the 3rd minute).
  • Once the collar is attached, you can finish sewing the facing to the front.
  • Turn right side out: the collar and the front-and-facing assembly are finished.

Body and sleeves

This is a standard set-in sleeve, explained in many videos online. The critical point is not to mix up the right and left sleeves: follow the safari jacket guide.

Lining (lined version)

  • Join the back lining and the front linings, shoulders then sides, and the sleeve linings.
  • Hem the lining: fold 1 cm, then 1 cm again, and stitch. The lining stops at the hips: it does not go down into the skirt, which keeps the dress light and easy to wear.
  • Attach the lining as for the lined safari jacket, along the facings and the neckline.

Tie belt

  • Join the two belt halves at the centre back.
  • Fold the belt in half lengthwise, right sides together, and stitch, leaving a 10 cm opening in the middle.
  • Turn right side out, press, and close the opening with slip stitches.
  • For the loops: fold each loop in three lengthwise, topstitch, then attach it to the side seam (see "the body").

Tips

  • Length: check it on the test garment. The "lengthen" setting on the site lets you adjust it.
  • Lined midi: the shoulder yoke suits it well. It gives the shoulders more structure and balances a long dress.
  • Midi: a slit, at the side or at the back, makes walking easier.

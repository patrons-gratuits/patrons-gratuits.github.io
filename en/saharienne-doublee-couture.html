Lined safari jacket: sewing and assembly guide

This guide gives the assembly order of the lined safari jacket, from cutting to attaching the sleeves to the bodice, and then to the final assembly with the lining. Wherever possible, photos replace long explanations: “A picture is worth a thousand words”.

Fabric

For summer, choose a light fabric: linen, lightweight cotton, seersucker. For mid-season, a more substantial one: gabardine, heavy drill, wool twill.

1. Cutting

Once the pieces are cut, carefully transfer the marks:

For the pieces to be lined:

  • Left front and right front. To avoid ending up with two left fronts or two right fronts, lay the fronts face to face, with the buttonhole and button lines against each other. Put the buttonhole marks on the left front and the button marks on the right front (for a women’s jacket, it is the other way round), then mark the front of each piece with a distinctive mark.
  • The lower fold lines, front and back, the facings and the cuffs.
  • The position of the pockets, as you like and in your own dimensions.
  • The back darts.
  • The top and bottom armhole marks on Sleeve 1 and Sleeve 2.
  • A centre mark on the collar, at the central fold, on the bottom line (the less curved one).
  • On the front piece, the top mark of the button placket.

For the lining pieces:

  • A centre mark on the collar, at the central fold, on the bottom line (the less curved one).
  • The top and bottom armhole marks on Sleeve 1 lining and Sleeve 2 lining.

2. Overlocking

No overlocking, because of the lining.

3. Fusing the interfacing

  • The front interfacing goes on the front.
  • The collar interfacing goes on the upper collar piece, with the collar in place and folded. If you use two fabrics for the collar, the interfacing goes on the visible fabric.

4. Sewing and assembly

Pleats and small pieces

  • Sew the cuff pleat (2 cm (¾ in) fold, 1.5 cm (⅝ in) seam), then join the cuffs, sides (a) together, sides (b) together.
  • Join the collar on three sides, leaving the bottom open, then turn it right side out. Topstitching the three sides improves the result.

Preparing the slit

Preparing the pieces

Sleeves

  • Join the sleeve pieces and their lining. To avoid ending up with two right sleeves or two left sleeves, lay your pieces face to face, in mirror image (sleeve head against sleeve head).
  • If you add one or more sleeve pockets and/or a pen and glasses holder, now is the time: it will be harder later.
  • Join one side first, topstitch it if you wish, then join the other two sides and topstitch them. Topstitching is not compulsory, but it strengthens the seam and gives a particular finish (no topstitching on the lining pieces).
  • Attach the cuffs to the sleeves. Towards the inside if you add sleeve tabs (right sides together, then turn). Without sleeve tabs, towards the outside or the inside as you prefer; wrong sides together for outer cuffs.

Back

Sew the back darts.

Front

  • If your pattern has a front dart, sew it before attaching the pockets.
  • Position your pockets (the position shown on the pattern is only a guide) and sew them on.
  • Partly join the facings to the fronts, stopping the seam at the top mark of the button placket.
  • Turn right side out and press the fold well. No topstitching for now: if you want some, do it after attaching the collar.
  • Make the buttonholes.

Front and back: the bodice (shoulder piece, if you chose the option)

  • Join the two back pieces, then the two back lining pieces. Caution: sew from the top of the pieces down to the mark, cut the mark without going past the seam line, then trim both sides by cutting off the corners, on the front and the back.
  • Join the backs to the right front and the left front. Do the same for the matching linings.
  • If you planned topstitching, topstitch the shoulder seam and the side seams, with the topstitching directed towards the back (again, there is no need to topstitch the lining pieces).
  • Press the bottom fold of the Front and Back pieces well.

Bodice and collar

  • To avoid any confusion, the simplest is to watch a very clear explanatory video: attaching the collar (from the 3rd minute). It has no English subtitles, but it is very clear.
  • With the collar assembled, you can finish the seam of the facing with the front.
  • Turn right side out: the collar and the front-and-facing assembly are finished.

Joining the back slits to the back

The photos, taken in sequence, detail every step of the assembly. The quality of the assembly depends largely on respecting the seam allowances: do not hesitate to mark with an erasable felt-tip pen where your seam must start or stop, to avoid overshooting.

If you are not quite sure of yourself, practise on old fabric: cut two strips to simulate the portion of back and lining that receives the slit, and the two slit pieces.

Bodice and sleeves

This is a standard sleeve assembly, explained by many videos online. The critical point is to avoid confusing the right sleeve and the left sleeve.

  • With the bodice laid flat, right side up, position the sleeves and mark them with a distinctive sign (for example R and L).
  • Place the bodice turned inside out, with the button placket facing you.
  • Slide the sleeves in from the inside and pin the seam line. Follow the marks: sleeve top on the shoulder seam, sleeve bottom on the back-front seam.
  • Sew, then turn right side out and check your seams. If there are puckers, redo the seam where needed.
  • Press the seam towards the bodice and topstitch, if you wish, on the bodice side.
